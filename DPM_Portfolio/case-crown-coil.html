Crown & Coil

A concept website for a Pretoria-based braiding salon, designed to solve real booking and pricing transparency issues found in customer reviews of similar local salons.

ROLE

Solo UX/UI Designer

TYPE

Self-initiated concept project

TOOLS

Figma

OVERVIEW

Context

Crown & Coil is a concept redesign for a small, Pretoria-based braiding salon. The project was self-initiated, based on research into common frustrations faced by customers of similar local salons — including unclear pricing, appointment clashes, and no visibility into availability before booking. As the sole designer, I was responsible for the full process: researching the problem, defining the brand identity, and designing the homepage, service/pricing page, and contact/booking page.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Defining the problem

Small local salons often take bookings only via phone calls and WhatsApp, with no way for customers to see real-time availability or pricing upfront. This leads to recurring issues: appointment clashes, long unexplained waits, and pricing disputes when the final cost differs from what was verbally quoted. A lightweight, conversion-focused website could address these gaps while staying simple enough for a small, non-technical team to manage.

RESEARCH

Grounding the design in real complaints

I researched real braiding salons in Soweto and read through their public Google reviews, which surfaced clear recurring patterns: appointment clashes with no plan to manage customers, clients waiting hours past their booking time with no communication, and pricing disputes where the final charge differed from the quoted price. These findings directly shaped the problem statement and feature priorities.

  • Competitive audit of real local salon reviews
  • Comparative look at how other local salons present pricing and booking
DESIGN

Bringing warmth and transparency together

My goal was to create a visual identity that felt warm and community-rooted rather than corporate. I designed primarily for mobile, since most customers would browse or book on their phones, using a warm color palette and real-feeling photography to build trust. Key decisions — like surfacing pricing and "this week's availability" directly on the homepage — trace straight back to the pricing disputes and appointment clashes found in the research.

Transparent pricing Visible availability Trust & social proof Lead capture form
USABILITY TESTING

Informal feedback

I shared the high-fidelity prototype with two friends for informal feedback. While no major usability issues surfaced, this was a lightweight, informal check rather than structured testing — a more rigorous round with people closer to the actual target audience would be a valuable next step.

LEARNINGS & NEXT STEPS

Reflection

Reading real customer complaints about local salons gave me a clearer, more specific problem to design for than if I'd guessed at pain points. It also taught me that vague feedback requests don't work — I'd ask more specific, targeted questions in a future testing round.

Next steps: structured usability testing with real potential customers, a desktop version of the site, and a simple SMS/WhatsApp booking confirmation flow.
