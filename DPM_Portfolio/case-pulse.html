Pulse by Peloyaka

A concept e-commerce site for an emerging premium headphone brand, designed to compete against giants like Beats, Sony, and Bang & Olufsen.

Solo UX/UI Designer

TYPE

Self-initiated concept project

TOOLS

Figma

OVERVIEW

Context

As a solo UX/UI designer, I worked on Pulse by Peloyaka, a concept e-commerce site for an emerging premium headphone brand positioned between Beats and Bang & Olufsen. My contribution included researching competitor positioning, designing the full visual identity, and building the homepage, product features, checkout, and confirmation flow in Figma.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Defining the problem

Emerging premium audio brands competing against giants like Beats, Sony, and Bang & Olufsen need an e-commerce experience that immediately communicates brand identity and quality, while still delivering the frictionless purchase flow larger competitors have trained customers to expect.

RESEARCH

Competitive positioning

Beats leans style and bass-forward, deeply tied to youth culture and the Apple ecosystem. Bang & Olufsen leans premium materials and design-as-decor, appealing to affluent professionals. Sony leans dependable, technically trustworthy quality across every price tier.

Pulse sits between Beats and B&O — style-forward like Beats, but with calmer, more premium materials and tone, without going as formal or expensive as B&O.

DESIGN

Translating positioning into UI

My goal was to create a visual identity aligned with the brand's motto, "Your heartbeat, amplified" — soft, premium, and lifestyle-driven rather than loud or corporate. I used a soft lavender palette and clean typography to reflect that middle ground between Beats and B&O. I designed primarily for mobile, since that's where most lifestyle e-commerce browsing happens, keeping layouts simple and image-led.

For the checkout flow specifically, I chose a single-page design over a multi-step one to reduce the risk of cart abandonment — a well-known e-commerce UX principle — rather than adding unnecessary friction.

USABILITY TESTING

Process

I created the wireframes directly in Figma, without an initial paper-sketch stage, then shared them with a friend and classmates from my design course for quick feedback before moving to high-fidelity screens.

LEARNINGS & NEXT STEPS

Reflection

Grounding the visual identity in real competitor research — rather than just picking colors I liked — made the design decisions much easier to defend and explain. It also showed me how much a brand's positioning shapes every downstream UI choice, from palette to tone of copy.

Next steps: add a dedicated product detail page (currently the flow jumps from features grid straight to checkout), and test the checkout flow with real users to see if the single-page approach actually reduces drop-off as intended.
