NaTIS Learner's License Booking

Redesigning the online booking flow for learner's license tests — solving the trade-off between travel distance and slot availability, and the stress of racing to grab a spot.

ROLE

Solo UX/UI Designer

TYPE

Self-initiated concept project

TOOLS

Figma

OVERVIEW

Context

Learners trying to book a learner's license test through NaTIS's online booking system face two major frustrations: available test slots are often concentrated at licensing departments far from where people live, and slots at nearby, in-demand departments get taken almost instantly — requiring users to repeatedly refresh the page and act within seconds. This turns booking into a stressful, luck-based process rather than a predictable one.

As the sole designer on this self-initiated project, I was responsible for the full process — from personal experience audit through to final screens.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Defining the problem

Learners booking through NaTIS face a trade-off between travel distance and wait time, and a system that rewards speed over fairness. This creates anxiety and excludes people who can't be online at the exact moment a slot opens — a real accessibility gap in a service everyone eventually needs.

RESEARCH

Grounding the redesign in real experience

Since I've personally been through this booking process, I started with a detailed account of my own journey — every step, every point of friction. I then looked at social media and forums for other people's complaints about NaTIS booking, which confirmed this wasn't just my experience but a widely shared one.

I also looked at how other high-demand, limited-availability booking problems are solved elsewhere — concert ticket queue systems and vaccine slot booking platforms from the COVID era — for patterns on handling scarcity fairly.

  • Personal experience audit of the current booking flow
  • Social listening across complaints about NaTIS booking
  • Comparable systems audit (ticket queues, vaccine booking)
DESIGN

The core idea

Rather than just making the existing flow prettier, I focused on one core bet: replacing the manual refresh-and-race pattern with a fairer notification-based system. Instead of requiring users to constantly check for open slots, the redesign lets users set a preferred radius and get notified the moment a matching slot opens — removing the need to be glued to a screen at the right second.

The flow covers onboarding and login, department and test type selection, the improved slot-finding interface, and a confirmation screen.

LEARNINGS & NEXT STEPS

Reflection

Designing around a real, lived frustration made the problem statement much sharper than starting from a hypothetical brief would have. It also reinforced that fairness, not just speed, is a legitimate design goal for booking systems under scarcity.

Next steps would include usability testing with people who've recently booked a learner's license test, and exploring how the notification system would need to adapt for users without reliable data or smartphone access.
